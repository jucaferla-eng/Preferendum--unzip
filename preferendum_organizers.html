The core insight

Decisions emerge
in the space
between extremes.

A binary poll asks "A or B?" — that's a false choice. Real preferences are gradients. Preferendum's cascading question trees let you map the full preference space of any population, revealing where collective decisions naturally land between the extremes you define.

🌳
Cascading question trees
After Q1, each answer branches into Q2. Each Q2 answer branches into Q3. You map the full decision topology of your market — not just the peak.
⚖️
Marginal Rate of Substitution
Quantify exactly how much of attribute A people sacrifice to gain attribute B. Taste vs. sugar. Growth vs. pollution. Tenure vs. accountability. The number that makes strategy precise.
🌍
Geographic preference gradients
Run the same consultation in Brazil and the USA simultaneously. The preference map differs. Send the right color to São Paulo, the right sole to New York. Before you manufacture a single unit.
🎁
Reward your participants
Companies that ask can give back. Offer a discount code, early access, or a product sample to everyone who helps you decide what to produce. Better data. Higher engagement. Loyal customers before launch.
How it works

From question to
market intelligence.

1
Design the tree
Define Q1 and its options. For each answer, optionally branch into Q2, then Q3. You're mapping a preference space, not running a poll.
2
People deliberate
Participants read the context, debate, and form opinions. The crowd intelligence sharpens before the vote is cast.
3
They navigate
Each voter follows their own branch — answering only the questions relevant to their Q1 choice. Every path is anonymous, encrypted, and blockchain-anchored.
4
You receive a map
Not a winner. A preference map. You see how the population distributes across the space — and exactly where to position your product, policy, or decision.
